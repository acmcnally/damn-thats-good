Baloo 2 + Nunito  —  closely related, warm

Both rounded; Baloo 2 is the louder cousin of Nunito. Lowest-risk pairing — they already look like family, so the split reads as emphasis rather than contrast. Chunky personality on the name and recipe titles; Nunito keeps lists and method calm.

Grandma’s Sunday Pot Roast

Serves 6 · 3 hr · from Mom’s recipe box

Ingredients

  • 3 lb beef chuck roast, patted dry
  • 1 large yellow onion, sliced thin
  • 4 medium carrots, in 2-inch pieces
  • 1½ cups dry red wine
  • 2 cups beef stock
  • 2 sprigs fresh thyme

Method

Season the roast generously and sear on all sides in a hot Dutch oven until deeply browned. Set aside, soften the onion and carrots in the fat, then deglaze with the wine.

Quicksand + Figtree  —  more contrast, more “brand”

Quicksand’s round geometric name reads distinctive and a little elegant; Figtree keeps the working UI crisp and highly legible. The two are less alike, so the display font feels like a logo and the rest feels like a tool. Quicksand’s weakness (light body text) never shows because it’s display-only here.

Fraunces + Nunito  —  a serif, but only for the name

Putting a soft, slightly quirky serif only on the wordmark and recipe titles is the classic cookbook / magazine-masthead move — it was the original “warm and personal” instinct. The earlier objection was “serif for everything”; this isn’t that. All working text stays Nunito. Highest “family recipe book” signal of the three; also the biggest visual step from the current all-sans mockup.
