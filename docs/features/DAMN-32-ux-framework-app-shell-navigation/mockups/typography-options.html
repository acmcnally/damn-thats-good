Nunito — the current pick

Rounded humanist. Soft terminals, generous x-height, wide weight range (200–1000). Reads friendly without being childish; long ingredient lists stay easy to scan. This is the reference the others are judged against.

Grandma’s Sunday Pot Roast

Serves 6 · 3 hr · from Mom’s recipe box

Ingredients

  • 3 lb beef chuck roast, patted dry
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, sliced thin
  • 4 medium carrots, in 2-inch pieces
  • 3 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 1½ cups dry red wine
  • 2 cups beef stock
  • 2 sprigs fresh thyme

Method

Season the roast generously and sear on all sides in a hot Dutch oven until deeply browned. Set aside, soften the onion and carrots in the fat, add the garlic for the last minute, then deglaze with the wine.

Rubik — lightly rounded, more structured

Geometric base with softly rounded corners — friendly, but reads a touch more grown-up / editorial than Nunito. Slightly narrower, so lists are more compact. Big weight range (300–900). Good if “warm but composed” beats “cozy.”

Baloo 2 — softest / chunkiest

Fully rounded, heavier-set. The most overtly “cozy recipe box” of the set — leans warm and casual. Only goes down to 400 (no light weight), and the chunkiness can crowd long lists at body size. Great personality for the wordmark; test whether it tires at paragraph length.

Figtree — friendly, a touch more refined

Geometric humanist built for UI. Friendly proportions and open apertures, but corners are crisp rather than rounded — warmth from shape, not from soft terminals. Excellent at small sizes; the most “app-native” of the set. Weights 300–900.

Quicksand — geometric, airy (likely too light for body)

Round geometric, very distinctive. Lovely for a wordmark, but the low contrast and light construction make long ingredient lists and method paragraphs harder to scan, and it only goes to 700. Included for completeness — probably a display-only font, not a whole-UI font.
